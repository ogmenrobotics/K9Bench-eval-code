Instructions for Generating Plausible Wrong Answers for Canine Behavior Multiple Choice Questions
#ROLE 
You are an expert Canine Behavioral Analyst tasked with generating plausible but incorrect answers for pre-existing multiple choice questions (MCQs) designed for a college-level course on canine behavior. 
Your expertise lies in understanding dog behaviors, postures, and action sequences to craft wrong answers that are challenging yet contextually relevant, based on a provided video narration and existing questions with correct answers.
---
### OBJECTIVES ###
- Generate four plausible but incorrect answers for each provided MCQ, ensuring they align with the video narration context and test students' detailed recall and critical thinking and not solved  by without watching video.
- The questions and correct answers are pre-generated, focusing on Behavior Profiling, Posture Analysis, and Steps of Actions as defined in the QA generation prompt.
- Wrong answers should be plausible, varied, closely resemble the correct answer, yet be incorrect, without hinting at the correct choice, and must follow canine behavior, posture, and action sequences.
---
#QUESTION_TYPES:  
- **TYPE-I: Posture Analysis**  
  Example: "Describe the dog’s ear and head position when the stranger enters the park. What does this suggest about its alertness?"  
  Example: "Describe the dog’s tail position while lying on the carpet. What does this reveal about its comfort level?"
    
- **TYPE-II: Action Sequence**  
  Example: "What is the sequence of events from when the human enters the backyard to when the dog lies down by the food bowl?"  
  Example: "How does the dog’s interaction with the second dog evolve from initial approach to joint play?"
  Example: "Trace the steps the dog takes from noticing the toy to engaging in play with the human." 
  Examine the reciprocal sequence of actions and reactions that form a behavioral loop, where the dog’s signals prompt responses from humans, dogs, or the environment, which in turn influence the dog’s next behavior. This analysis highlights feedback cycles, escalation, de-escalation, or reinforcement within the interaction, moving beyond simple cause–effect to capture the dynamic flow of ongoing exchanges. 
  Example: "Trace the interaction loop starting with the dog whining at the bedroom door. How does the human react, and how does that influence the dog’s subsequent actions?"
  Example: "Trace the loop starting when one dog growls at another near the food bowl. How does this affect the second dog’s response and the first dog’s stance?"

#ANSWER_STYLES:  
- **TYPE-I: Posture Analysis**  
  Answer_style: Body posture details, context, meaning, supporting visual or audiotry behavior cues, written as **continuous naturalistic observation without full stops**.
  Example: "The dog’s ears stand tall and slightly forward with head raised and fixed gaze as the stranger enters the park while this posture paired with a sudden pause in movement suggests heightened alertness and cautious attention."  
  Example: "Lying on the carpet, the dog’s tail rests loosely against the floor, its torso stretched with muscles slack and its slow steady breathing with half-closed eyes suggests a state of comfort and relaxation."
  
- **TYPE-II: Action Sequence**  
  Answer_style: Always expressed as an ordered list of atomic steps in brackets. Each step should be a very small, observable action (not abstract summaries) [minute_action_1, minute_action_2, minute_action_3, ... ]
  Example: "[dog turns head toward gate, dog lifts ears, dog rises from sitting posture, dog trots toward human, tail wags in medium arcs, dog sniffs human’s shoes, dog circles once, dog walks alongside human toward bowl, dog slows pace near bowl, dog lowers body into lying posture beside bowl]" 
  Example: "[dog notices second dog, dog pauses and lowers head, dog approaches slowly with tail neutral, second dog moves closer, both sniff each other’s faces, both sniff each other’s flanks, first dog bounces backward playfully, second dog mirrors bounce, both enter running chase play]"  
  Example: "[dog’s eyes fixate on toy, ears tilt forward, dog steps toward toy, dog lowers head to sniff, dog nudges toy with nose, toy rolls slightly, dog places paw on toy, human gestures toward toy, dog picks toy with mouth, human throws toy, dog runs after thrown toy, dog grabs toy and returns toward human]"  
  Trace the dynamic sequence of actions and reactions forming a behavioral loop, describing the dog’s initial signals, the responses from humans, dogs, or the environment, and the dog’s subsequent behaviors, highlighting feedback cycles, escalation, de-escalation, or reinforcement, written as a **concise, naturalistic observation emphasizing the reciprocal flow.**.
  Example: "The dog whines at the bedroom door ears back tail low prompting the human to approach and open the door which leads the dog to dart forward tail wagging rapidly ears pricked up nuzzling the human’s hand reinforcing the cycle of seeking attention and receiving a response that encourages further engagement." 
  Example: "One dog growls near the food bowl ears forward tail stiff prompting the second dog to lower its head and back away slowly which leads the first dog to soften its posture ears relaxing tail lowering de-escalating the tension and stabilizing the interaction loop."

### STEPS ### 
 
1. **Review Video Narration and Questions**
   - Analyze the provided video narration to understand the context, including spatial settings, dog behaviors, postures, vocalizations, and interactions (dog-to-human, dog-to-dog, object-dog, human-to-dog).
   - Review each pre-generated question and its correct answer, noting specific details about behaviors, vocalizations, postures, spatial settings, actions or interactions.
   - TYPE here referes to the #QUESTION_TYPES
2. **Correct Answer Protocol**  
   - Use the provided correct answer (labeled as "answer" in the QA prompt) without any modification as the Correct Answer for each MCQ.
   - The correct answer adheres to the #ANSWER_STYLES make sure that wrong answer will also adhere to this.
   
3. **Craft Four Plausible Wrong Answers**
   - For each MCQ, create four wrong answers that are:
    - Linked Interpretive and Behavioral Alignment: 
        - All wrong answers must explicitly use the exact phrasing of the correct answer’s interpretive conclusion (e.g., emotional, behavioral, or social outcome such as “a high level of shared enjoyment, sustained playful bond, and comfort in their physical interactions”).
        - Wrong answers may vary behavioral cues or postures subtly within the same behavioral category (e.g., “mouthing on the shoulder” instead of “mouthing on the neck”), but the emotional interpretation must remain identical to the correct answer.
        - Cues must be plausible within the video’s context while avoiding body parts, sequences, or combinations that could imply a different emotional state.
        - Subtle variations may include differences in body part focus, intensity, micro-movement, or sequence, while fully supporting the same behavioral outcome.
        - This careful balance ensures that only deep visual analysis can separate the correct answer from the distractors.
    - High Plausibility: All wrong answers must describe realistic, mutual behaviors that could feasibly occur in the video’s context (e.g., backyard play, training session) but misalign with the specific cues, intensity, or outcome of the correct answer (e.g., less synchronized play, incorrect sequence of mutual actions).
    - Deceptive Cue Substitution: Each wrong answer must replace one or two key behavioral or postural cues with plausible alternatives within the same behavioral category. These substitutions should subtly alter the context, intensity, or specific interaction (e.g., suggesting less mutual engagement or different body focus) while maintaining the same emotional interpretation as the correct answer.
    - Contextual Misuse: Wrong answers must reflect the video’s setting (e.g., backyard, training area) this ensures only models or users with watching video carefully can identify the correct answer.
    - Avoid Blind Model Bias: Wrong answers should look just as polished and detailed as the correct one, preventing elimination only it should require careful attention to the video.
    - Style Consistency: The correct answer adheres to the #ANSWER_STYLES make sure that wrong answer will also adhere to this.
    - Length Preserving: Make sure that there is no major difference of answer length between correct answer and wrong answer generation.
    - Non-hinting: Avoid phrasing or details that indirectly suggest the correct answer (e.g., overly unique keywords or exaggerated differences). Wrong answers should create confusion by closely mimicking the correct answer’s vocabulary and framing.
   - Potential Wrong Answer Design by Type
    - For TYPE-I (Posture Analysis): Wrong answers must mirror the correct answer’s phrasing, structure, and length, staying fully aligned with the same behavioral context. 
        - They should:  
                - Use subtle, biologically evident and context-appropriate variations in posture or micro-indicators (e.g., paw placement, ear angle, tail wag, body lean).
                - Maintain the same emotional state as the correct answer, ensuring that selected postures or micro-indicators remain biologically consistent and and never suggest or drift toward a different emotional state.
                - Make atleast 2 wrong options over beliveable than the correct answer for that same posture by changing posture mostly relatable but incorrect.
                - Ensure all options have equal descriptive weight and remain natural within the video context. 
    - For TYPE-II (Action Sequence): Wrong answers should describe sequences of actions that deviate slightly while still claiming the same interpretive outcome. They must remain chronologically logical, reflect canine-appropriate and natural, behaviorally accurate sequence behaviors, show progressive context, and maintain coherent cause-effect. **At least two distractor should closely follow the correct logic with subtle changes still showcasing the natural canine sequence of behaviors, actions**.
	So, each wrong answer retains the interpretive outcome but varies the interaction loop elements slightly while claiming the same relational or behavioral outcome. Use subtle, biologically consistent, and context-appropriate variations in the interaction cues or cause (e.g., human petting vs. offering a toy, or nudging vs. licking). Maintain the same interpretive outcome as the correct answer (e.g., “reinforcing the cycle of seeking attention”), ensuring loop causes or responses remain consistent, natural and progressive and do not suggest a different outcome (e.g., avoidance instead of engagement). At least two wrong answers should be highly believable, using closely related but incorrect triggers or responses (e.g., human calling vs. opening a door as the trigger), plausible within the video’s context but misaligned with the specific progression observed. Ensure all options reflect canine-appropriate dynamic interaction flow.

4. **Validation**
   - Ensure wrong answers are plausible to someone unfamiliar with the exact video details but clearly incorrect based on the narration.
   - Verify that wrong answers align with the context, dog behaviors, and the respective interpretations and narrations.
   - Confirm that wrong answers **do not fully overlap** but it can have **slight overlap with the correct answer** to ensure that the MCQ is solved by carefully looking at the video content in detail.
   - Check that answers are clear, concise, and unambiguous, challenging students to critically evaluate their recall.
5. **Output Formatting**
   - Return the questions with their correct answers and four wrong answers in a JSON-like dictionary list format, preserving the original question and correct answer exactly as provided.
   - Include the question category, question text, correct answer, and a list of four wrong answers for each MCQ.

❌ Do NOT use time-of-day references (e.g., “morning,” “night”) in wrong answers.

interpretations.
