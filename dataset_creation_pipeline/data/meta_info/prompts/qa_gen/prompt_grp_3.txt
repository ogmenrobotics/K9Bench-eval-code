Instructions for Generating Canine State and Purpose Understanding Questions and Answers

#ROLE
You are an expert Canine Behavioral Analyst specializing in generating advanced examination questions and answers that assess deep observation, reasoning, and interpretation of canine emotional and purposeful behavior.
Your expertise lies in decoding emotional states, contextual influences, causal behavioral sequences, and social interaction patterns in dogs across extended video observations.

---

### OBJECTIVE ###
- You are tasked with creating **1 to 8 highly challenging questions and answers** that test the **long-term understanding of canine emotional states, context-driven actions, and causal behavioral relationships**.
- The questions must evaluate a candidate’s ability to **recall, interpret, and connect emotional cues, context, and progression** across the entire observation, not just isolated moments.
- You must generate questions under the following **three analytical types** (**skip a type only if it is genuinely incompatible with the observed video content**):  

#QUESTION_TYPES:  
- **TYPE-I: Context Analysis**  
  Evaluate how environmental or social context influences dog behavior or decision-making.
  Example: "How does the presence of the open door affect the dog’s persistence in whining at the entrance?"
  Example: "Why does the dog adjust its path when another dog suddenly enters its play area?"
  Example: "What environmental cues triggered the excitement in a dog?"
  Example: "In a training scenario, how does a dog’s body language reflect its confidence in performing a task?"
  Example: "How does leash restriction alter the dog’s behavior when an unfamiliar dog enters the park?"
    
- **TYPE-II: Causal Effect Analysis** 
  Focus on cause-effect relationships between actions, events, or signals. 
  Example: "What event immediately precedes the dog barking at the hallway door?"
  Example: "What event immediately triggers the dog to nudge its owner repeatedly?"
  Example: "What event immediately causes the dog to stop playing and lie down?"
  Example: "How does the dog react after the treat bag is opened?"
  Example: "How does the dog’s circling behavior change after the other pet enters the room?"  

---

#ANSWER_STYLES:  
- **TYPE-I: Context Analysis**  
  Answer_style: Detail how context or setting influences behavior, incorporating spatial cues, object affordances, and social interactions, written as **continuous naturalistic observation without full stops**.
  Example: "The dog pauses at the doorway, ears forward, tail neutral, adjusting its path around the newcomer showing cautious attention to the presence of another dog."  
  Example: "With the open door nearby, the dog stands with head raised, ears alert, tail slightly wagging, repeatedly looking toward the entrance while staying near the human reflecting heightened curiosity and vigilance."
  Example: "In a training session, the dog aligns its body toward the trainer, ears pricked forward, tail neutral, movements deliberate suggesting confidence and responsiveness to environmental cues."
    
- **TYPE-II: Causal Effect Analysis** 
  Answer_style: Describe the observed behavior and its immediate trigger or causal event, written as **concise, naturalistic observation, emphasizing cause-effect reasonings**.
  Example: "After the vacuum cleaner starts, the dog freezes, tucks its tail, and backs away toward its bed showing caution and avoidance." 
  Example: "The human gestures toward the toy and the dog’s eyes fixate, ears tilt forward, and it steps toward the toy reflecting interest and readiness to engage."  
  Example: "The dog hears the treat bag rustle and immediately lifts its head, pricks its ears forward, and trots toward the human signaling anticipation of reward."
